# Max 5x Wins the $100 Coding Showdown

Upgrade to Claude Max 5x. In October 2026 limit testing it gives you the same Claude Code capacity as five Pro accounts: about **$5,725 versus $5,890 a month** of API-priced Opus 5.5 usage, a gap inside the ±5% measurement error. It avoids everything that makes juggling accounts painful: separate settings and memory, a cold prompt cache after every switch, reasoning that some models silently drop, chat history spread over five logins, and an open terms-of-service question. It also includes Fable 5.1, up to half the weekly limit, and $100 a month of API credits, though those credits buy no interactive Claude Code time. OpenAI's $100 ChatGPT Pro tier is the "Codex Pro 5x" plan, and OpenAI doubled its Codex allowance on October 29, 2026. Measured in API-priced usage, Max 5x carries **about 4x as much before the doubling and about 2x after it**, if the doubling fully takes effect. Adjusting for token efficiency and intelligence cuts both ways. At frontier quality (Opus 5.5 against GPT-6 Astra), Claude keeps an **edge of about 1.9x**. A Codex user who mostly runs GPT-6.1 Sol, a much cheaper model, would get **about 1.7–2.4x more mid-tier work** done, but only if the doubling holds, and that change is one day old and unmeasured. Astra has overtaken Fable 5.1 on independent benchmarks but only ties Opus 5.5, Claude Code's default model. Switching to Codex therefore buys no gain in intelligence, and it costs a workflow migration plus either $120 a month (to keep Claude Pro) or Opus chat (if Claude drops to Free). Codex is the better buy only if you code in long bursts, are happy to run Sol for most work, and are willing to bet the doubling delivers.

## Five Pro accounts now equal one Max 5x, so the extras decide

Anthropic defines Max 5x by its five-hour window: "five times the Pro plan's per-session usage allowance." Both plans also have a weekly cap across all models, and Anthropic does not publish its size ([Help Center: What is the Max plan?](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11049741-what-is-the-max-plan)). The **3.5x weekly ratio** you remember is real but out of date. It comes from August 2025, when Anthropic published weekly ranges of 40–80 hours of Sonnet 4 for Pro and 140–280 hours for the $100 Max plan ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/28/anthropic-unveils-new-rate-limits-to-curb-claude-code-power-users/)). The same ratio underpins *Kahn v. Anthropic*, a proposed class action filed in June 2026, which alleges that Max 5x delivered only about 3.5x Pro. Those are allegations, not findings ([ChatGPT Is Eating the World](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/06/15/karl-kahn-sues-anthropic-in-class-action-for-alleged-misrepresentation-and-false-advertising-in-promoting-max-20x-plans-for-claude-allegedly-as-delivering-twenty-times-greater-than-usage-lim/)). Limits have changed several times since. Anthropic removed Opus-specific caps and raised Max limits in November 2025 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-5)), reportedly doubled five-hour limits in May 2026 ([explainx.ai](https://explainx.ai/blog/claude-usage-limits-2026-timeline-explained)), and raised five-hour limits again when Opus 5.5 launched on September 22, 2026 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5)).

The best current measurement is SemiAnalysis's October 2026 limit test on Opus 5.5. The report is paywalled, so these figures come from explainx.ai's summary of it. It values a fully used Pro account at **$1,178 a month** of API-priced usage and Max 5x at **$5,725**, a ratio of about **4.9x** ([explainx.ai summary of SemiAnalysis](https://explainx.ai/blog/semianalysis-claude-vs-chatgpt-subscription-api-value-limit-testing-2026)). Five Pro accounts therefore come to about $5,890. That is roughly 3% more than one Max 5x, which falls inside the study's ±5% tracking error, so the two are effectively tied. Five Pros edge ahead at all only because the September increase favored Pro: SemiAnalysis measured about 50% more tokens on Pro against about 20% more on Max.

Wokey sells API access, so it has a commercial interest here. Its gateway data prices real traffic across several models rather than synthetic runs. Its absolute values are lower (Pro at about $681 a month), but the pattern is the same. Wokey's data also shows that the plan now falling short of its label is Max 20x, which gives roughly 10x Pro per week, not 20x ([Wokey](https://wokey.ai/claude-usage-limits)). Wokey finds that Pro's weekly cap only binds after about 15 full five-hour windows. So if you keep running out on Pro, you are most likely hitting the five-hour window, which is exactly what Max 5x multiplies.

Since capacity is a wash, the extras decide, and almost all of them favor Max. **Fable 5.1 is excluded from Pro limits entirely** and runs only on usage credits at API rates, while Max includes it up to 50% of the weekly limit ([Help Center: Fable models on your plan](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15424964-claude-fable-models-on-your-plan)). SemiAnalysis values that allowance at about $1,273 a month, but its figures also imply that Fable uses roughly 4.5 times as much of the limit per token as Opus 5.5, so it is a capped bonus rather than a reason to upgrade. Max also gets the "wrapping up" grace, which lets Claude Code finish a response when a five-hour limit hits mid-answer, every time it happens; Pro gets it once a week ([Help Center: Claude Code with Pro or Max](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11145838-use-claude-code-with-your-pro-or-max-plan)). Because Claude chat and Claude Code draw on the same allowance, Max's single large pool also absorbs chat that would otherwise eat into one Pro account's coding budget. Max further includes $100 a month of API credits, covered below.

Five Pro accounts win only on price. Billed annually, Pro costs $200 a year ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)), so five accounts work out to about $83 a month. That saving means committing a year of payments to accounts whose standing under the terms is unsettled.

| | Five Claude Pro accounts | Claude Max 5x | ChatGPT Pro $100 (Codex) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly cost | $100 (about $83 billed annually) | $100 | $100, or $120 keeping Claude Pro |
| API-priced usage per month | ≈$5,890 on Opus 5.5 | ≈$5,725 on Opus 5.5 | ≈$1,400 measured before Oct 29; ≈$2,800 if the doubling holds |
| Limit shape | Five separate 5-hour windows and weekly caps | One window at 5x Pro; weekly ≈4.9x Pro | Weekly cap only, no 5-hour window |
| Top models included | Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 (Fable only at API rates) | Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Fable 5.1 (≤50% of weekly) | GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol |
| Extras | None | $100/month API credits, not usable in interactive Claude Code | ChatGPT Pro chat features |
| Hidden cost | Config sync, cache and reasoning loss per switch, terms risk | None beyond price | Workflow migration; Claude history stays behind |

## Juggling accounts breaks far more than chat and caching

Claude.ai and prompt caching are real problems, but they are only two of at least six. The others are configuration drift, lost reasoning, tooling quirks and terms-of-service risk.

### Settings, skills, transcripts and memory stay behind unless synced

Your setup carries across accounts unchanged only if you keep a single config directory and swap credentials in place. Anthropic documents exactly one multi-account method: give each account its own `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`, for example through a shell alias. In the docs' words, "each directory has its own settings, session history, and claude.ai login or API key" ([Authentication docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/authentication)). That directory holds everything global: your user CLAUDE.md and rules, `settings.json`, skills, commands, agents, output styles, plugins, personal MCP servers in `.claude.json`, transcripts under `projects/<project>/`, **auto memory** under `projects/<project>/memory/`, and prompt history ([.claude directory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-directory)). Only files committed to the repo (the project CLAUDE.md, `.claude/` skills, agents and settings, and `.mcp.json`) work under every account automatically. Everything else needs symlinks or scripts, and people use both. One recipe from Claude Code's main multi-account issue symlinks `settings.json`, `skills/`, `commands/`, `agents/` and the plugin folders across directories. It keeps `.claude.json` and `.credentials.json` separate, because swapping those two is "not parallel-safe." Plugin manifests also contain absolute paths, so they need their own copy per directory ([GitHub #30031](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/30031)).

The alternative is to swap credentials in place with a tool such as claude-swap (about 4.3k stars). It keeps one `~/.claude` directory, so everything except the login carries over. Its README warns against running `/logout` before switching, because Claude Code "may revoke the refresh token" ([claude-swap](https://github.com/realiti4/claude-swap)).

A third option looks simpler still: launch each process with its own one-year token from `claude setup-token`, passed in `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN`, over one shared directory. No one has documented that combination, so test it before relying on it ([Authentication docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/authentication)).

Claude Code has no built-in way to switch accounts. The feature request has been open since March 2026, with about 79 upvotes and no reply from Anthropic staff. The same thread documents the rough edges: the Desktop app ignores `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`, the VS Code extension can leak session lists across accounts, and IDEs that launch `claude` without loading your shell profile silently use the default account. Most awkwardly, according to one user's reading of the binary, a running session caches its access token and does not reload credentials after a 429 rate-limit error, so a swap takes effect only when the token refreshes or the session restarts ([GitHub #30031](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/30031)).

The claude.ai side splits as well. Claude chat and Claude Code share one usage allowance ([Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11145838-use-claude-code-with-your-pro-or-max-plan)), so chatting on an account uses up that account's coding budget, and each account keeps its own chat history and projects. Cloud Claude Code sessions can be moved to the terminal only by the account that started them ([Claude Code on the web](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-code-on-the-web)).

### Every switch cold-starts the cache and can erase reasoning

Prompt caches are "isolated between organizations" ([Prompt caching API docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)). Claude Code's cache is also "effectively scoped to one machine and directory," because the system prompt includes the auto-memory path ([Prompt caching in Claude Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/prompt-caching)). An account switch is therefore a full cache miss.

The next request rewrites the whole context at the one-hour cache-write price, which is **2x the base input price**. A normal cached read on Opus 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5 costs 0.05x. One switch therefore costs about **40 times** what re-reading the context normally costs, and about 80 times on Fable 5.1.

At list prices, rewriting a 120,000-token Opus 5.5 context costs about $0.96, against $0.024 to re-read it ([Claude API pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). That is roughly 9% of the ~$11 of usage Wokey measures per Pro five-hour window. The estimate assumes subscription meters weight cache writes the way the API prices them, which Anthropic does not disclose.

The cost lands on the account you switch to. Switching back doesn't recover the old cache, because a five-hour limit takes longer to reset than the one-hour cache lasts.

Reasoning continuity suffers too. Per the docs, "Thinking blocks that Claude Sonnet 5.5 or Claude Haiku 5.5 produces work only in the account that produced them or in an account linked to it." If another account sends them, the API silently drops them and the request succeeds without that reasoning ([Preserved thinking docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/preserved-thinking)). Opus 5.5 and Fable thinking survives a switch unless the original account is no longer in good standing. Anthropic does not define what "linked" means for consumer accounts. Older GitHub reports of hard "Invalid signature in thinking block" errors after switching mostly involve proxies or moves between backends ([GitHub #21726](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/21726)).

The sessions themselves move without trouble: `claude --resume <absolute path>` reopens any transcript file ([Sessions docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sessions)). To keep switches cheap, switch right after `/compact` or `/clear`, or start a fresh session on the new account.

### The one-hour cache is a billing rule, not a tier perk

The five-minute versus one-hour split is neither Free-versus-Pro nor Pro-versus-Max. It separates subscription usage from pay-per-token usage, and the docs treat every subscription tier identically ([Prompt caching in Claude Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/prompt-caching)).

| Request type | Subscription, within plan limits | Usage credits, API key or cloud provider |
|---|---|---|
| Main conversation | **1 hour** | 5 minutes |
| Subagents, forks, compaction, titles | 5 minutes (server-controlled helpers get 1 hour) | 5 minutes |

Pro and Max therefore get identical caching. The main conversation drops to five minutes only when a subscriber exceeds plan limits and starts drawing on usage credits, which setting `ENABLE_PROMPT_CACHING_1H=1` reverses. From v2.1.242, `CLAUDE_CODE_PROMPT_CACHE_TTL` and `CLAUDE_CODE_SUBAGENT_PROMPT_CACHE_TTL` set each bucket explicitly ([Env vars reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/env-vars)), and `claude -p "hello" --output-format json` reports which cache lifetime a request actually wrote.

### A supervisor can route worker processes, not in-session subagents

Claude Code holds one credential per process. Its subagent controls are model-only (`CLAUDE_CODE_SUBAGENT_MODEL`), with no per-subagent account setting ([Env vars reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/env-vars)), and a request for built-in multi-account load balancing, filed by a user with four Max subscriptions, was closed by its author as "filed in error" without any staff reply ([GitHub #43978](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/43978)). A supervising agent therefore cannot hand an in-session subagent to another account, but it can launch separate `claude -p` or Agent SDK workers, each with its own config directory or token. It can read each account's remaining five-hour and seven-day allowance from the `rate_limits` fields Claude Code exposes to Pro and Max sessions after their first response ([Statusline docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/statusline)), or poll an undocumented usage endpoint that users report answers with persistent 429 errors ([GitHub #31021](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/31021)), and dispatch each task to whichever account has the most headroom. The cache penalty is small, because subagents never read the parent's cache and already run on the five-minute lifetime. Community tools automate the rotation: claude-swap switches accounts at 90% usage, and proxies such as ccflare load-balance several accounts behind `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` ([ccflare](https://github.com/snipeship/claudeflare)). Proxies, however, are precisely the pattern Anthropic's rules prohibit. On Max 5x, the sanctioned version of the same idea is to send headless worker jobs to the plan's API credits.

### Team shares neither cache nor transcripts, and Premium costs $120 minimum

Team does not solve the switching problem. Seats sit in one organization, but because Claude Code scopes its cache to one machine and directory, nothing indicates automatic cache reuse between members. Local transcripts never sync, and cloud sessions set to Team visibility are shared only as read-only snapshots that the owning account alone can continue ([Claude Code on the web](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-code-on-the-web)). Usage limits are per member, and Team requires at least two seats. Standard seats cost $25 a month ($20 billed annually) for 1.25x Pro per session; Premium seats cost $125 ($100 billed annually) for 6.25x Pro, which is 25% more per session than Max 5x, with Fable under the same 50% rule ([Help Center: Team plan](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9266767-what-is-the-team-plan)). The cheapest one-person Premium setup is therefore **$150 a month, or $120 on annual billing**, with an idle second seat and $120 of pooled API credits ([API credits docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/api-credits-for-subscribers)). Four Standard seats do reach 5x Pro per session for $100, but only by recreating the four-account juggle under Commercial Terms this research did not review. Team makes sense near this budget only if a colleague would genuinely use the second seat. One untested wrinkle remains: because the API isolates caches by organization and workspace, two seats in the same organization swapping credentials over a single config directory could in theory share a cache.

### The terms tolerate extra accounts but not limit-dodging

No Anthropic text found says "one account per person." The Consumer Terms (effective October 8, 2025) never mention multiple accounts or usage limits; they forbid credential sharing, automated access "except when you are accessing our Services via an Anthropic API Key or where we otherwise explicitly permit it," and "bypassing any of our systems or protective measures," and they allow suspension "without notice" ([Consumer Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)). Claude Code team member Thariq Shihipar wrote in February 2026 that "it's not against terms of service to have multiple MAX accounts," while reselling tokens is ([X](https://x.com/trq212/status/2024230184287949207)). Against that, the Claude Code legal page says advertised limits "assume ordinary, individual usage" ([Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)), and the Usage Policy version taking effect November 12, 2026 bars creating multiple accounts "to avoid detection, circumvent product guardrails," intentionally bypassing product "restrictions, or guardrails," and services "that route requests through consumer subscriptions" ([Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)). Documented 2026 enforcement targeted third-party harnesses, OAuth proxies, resale and fraud signals ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/20/anthropic_clarifies_ban_third_party_claude_access/)). No primary report shows a ban for rotating personally owned Pro accounts in the official Claude Code binary, and the most-cited article on multi-account bans rests on anecdotes ([MetricNexus](https://metricnexus.ai/blog/anthropic-banning-multiple-claude-accounts)). The risk is interpretive and enforcement is automated and opaque: five accounts on one machine, IP address and card, exhausting limits in rotation, especially under an automated supervisor, is an unusual pattern, and a ban could take every linked account at once. Max 5x carries none of that risk.
